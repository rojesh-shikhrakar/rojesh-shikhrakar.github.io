## Learning Objectives

By the end of the workshop, participants will be able to:

- Explain what generative and agentic AI can and cannot reliably do.
- Write prompts that provide sufficient context, constraints, examples, and evaluation criteria.
- Turn successful prompts into reusable skills for individuals and teams.
- Use connectors safely to ground AI in authorized workplace information.
- Design bounded agentic workflows with clear tools, limits, checkpoints, and escalation paths.
- Verify AI output and make risk-based decisions about human oversight.
- Apply privacy, security, fairness, transparency, copyright, and accountability principles in daily work.
- Measure productivity gains without sacrificing quality, trust, or employee well-being.

## A Practical Prompt Framework

Use **G-C-I-C-E-F** as a starting structure rather than a rigid formula:

1. **Goal:** State the outcome and why it matters.
2. **Context:** Explain the audience, situation, background, and relevant definitions.
3. **Inputs:** Provide the source material the model should use.
4. **Constraints:** Define boundaries, policies, tone, length, exclusions, and deadlines.
5. **Evaluation:** Describe what a good result must contain and how it will be checked.
6. **Format:** Specify the structure of the response.

## The Human–AI Workflow Canvas

Each capstone workflow should answer these design questions:

- **Outcome**: What useful result should the workflow produce?
- **Trigger**: What starts the workflow, and how often does it run?
- **Inputs**: Which sources are required, and who owns them?
- **AI**: Is AI drafting, transforming, retrieving, analyzing, recommending, or acting?
- **Tools**: Which models, skills, connectors, or applications may it use?
- **Boundaries**: What data, actions, or decisions are prohibited?
- **Verification**:Which facts, calculations, citations, and assumptions must be checked?
- **Approval**: Who reviews the output or authorizes an external action?
- **Failure**:When must the system stop, retry, revert, or escalate?
- **Evidence**:What should be logged to make the process traceable?
- **Measure** :How will time, quality, rework, cost, and risk be compared with the baseline?

## Choosing the Right Level of Automation

| Work pattern                                | Recommended approach                      | Example                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| One-off, low-risk task                      | Prompt with human review                  | Rewrite a paragraph for clarity                             |
| Recurring task with stable standards        | Reusable skill                            | Prepare a weekly status update                              |
| Task requiring authorized workplace context | Skill plus read-only connector            | Summarize project updates from approved sources             |
| Predictable sequence with fixed rules       | Traditional automation                    | Copy approved form data into a system                       |
| Variable, multi-step, reversible task       | Bounded agent with checkpoints            | Assemble a research brief from permitted sources            |
| High-impact or irreversible decision        | Human-led process with limited AI support | Hiring, disciplinary, legal, medical, or financial decision |

## Responsible AI Check Before You Use or Share an Output

Apply the **S-A-F-E-R** check:

- **Source:** Can important claims be traced to credible, current evidence?
- **Accuracy:** Have facts, calculations, quotations, and citations been verified?
- **Fairness:** Could the result disadvantage, exclude, stereotype, or misrepresent anyone?
- **Exposure:** Does the workflow reveal confidential, personal, copyrighted, or restricted information?
- **Responsibility:** Is a named person accountable for the decision, action, and correction of errors?

Higher-impact work requires stronger evidence, more testing, and more senior human review. If the consequences of an error are unclear, pause and escalate rather than increasing autonomy.

## Capstone Challenge (Optional)

In small groups, participants redesign one recurring workplace process and present it to the cohort. The final presentation must include:

1. The current process, users, pain points, and baseline effort.
2. The proposed human–AI workflow and expected benefit.
3. One reusable prompt or skill.
4. Any connectors, requested permissions, and data boundaries.
5. The agent’s tools, limits, approval gates, stop conditions, and fallback path.
6. A test set with normal, ambiguous, incomplete, and adversarial examples.
7. Risks related to accuracy, privacy, security, bias, copyright, and accountability.
8. Success metrics and a 30-day pilot plan.
