## The Research Workflow Challenge

Literature reviews require substantial time for searching, retrieving, deduplicating, screening, and organizing sources. These activities are essential, but they can crowd out the work that depends most on a researcher’s expertise: identifying gaps, evaluating methods, formulating hypotheses, and interpreting evidence.

This workshop shows participants how AI can reduce repetitive effort in the research workflow while keeping scholarly judgment and verification firmly in human hands.

## Tools Explored

- **Elicit:** Semantic paper discovery and structured extraction of methods and findings
- **Perplexity:** Current research discovery with linked sources and citation trails
- **ResearchRabbit:** Citation-network exploration, author mapping, and related-work discovery
- **Connected Papers:** Visual exploration of relationships among papers
- **Semantic Scholar:** Paper discovery, recommendations, and ongoing topic monitoring
- **NotebookLM:** Source-grounded, multi-document synthesis and knowledge organization
- **Claude:** Structured notes, analysis assistance, argument review, and writing refinement
- **Zotero:** Citation management, annotation, tagging, and research-library organization

## Academic Integrity Boundaries

AI can support structure, clarity, procedural drafting, code development, and source-grounded synthesis. It must not generate claims, findings, or conclusions that the researcher has not independently verified. It must not fabricate citations, replace the researcher’s interpretation of data, or supply the substantive intellectual contribution of a manuscript.

Because institutional, funder, and publisher policies differ and continue to evolve, participants learn how to check the rules that apply to their work, document their process, and disclose AI use appropriately.

## Prerequisites

Participants should be actively involved in research or enrolled in a graduate research program and should be comfortable using academic databases such as Google Scholar, PubMed, or an equivalent service. No programming experience is required.
