## Learning Objectives

By the end of the workshop, participants will be able to:

- Explain how ChatGPT works well enough to anticipate common strengths, limitations, and failure modes.
- Write and iteratively improve prompts using context, examples, constraints, and explicit quality standards.
- Choose appropriately among standard chat, Search, Deep Research, data analysis, Canvas, Projects, custom GPTs, apps, and automation.
- Work effectively with documents, images, spreadsheets, voice, and other multimodal inputs.
- Produce source-grounded research and verify that citations support the claims being made.
- Analyze data with transparent assumptions and independently check important calculations and interpretations.
- Build and test Projects and custom GPTs for dependable recurring work.
- Design connected and multi-step workflows with least-privilege access, checkpoints, and safe failure behavior.
- Apply privacy, security, fairness, accessibility, copyright, disclosure, and accountability principles in daily use.
- Measure whether ChatGPT improves real outcomes without sacrificing quality, trust, or human ownership.

## A Practical Prompt Framework

Use **G-C-I-C-E-F** as a flexible starting point:

1. **Goal:** State the result you need, the decision it supports, and why it matters.
2. **Context:** Describe the audience, situation, background, terminology, and relevant prior decisions.
3. **Inputs:** Supply or identify the material ChatGPT should use and distinguish authoritative sources.
4. **Constraints:** Define boundaries, policies, tone, length, dates, exclusions, tools, and prohibited assumptions.
5. **Evaluation:** Explain what a good result must contain, what must be verified, and how quality will be judged.
6. **Format:** Specify the desired structure, such as a table, memo, checklist, schema, or presentation outline.

For complex work, add a process instruction: ask ChatGPT to clarify missing information, propose a plan, complete one stage at a time, check its output against the evaluation criteria, and clearly mark uncertainty.

## The Human–ChatGPT Workflow Canvas

Every capstone workflow should answer:

- **Outcome:** What useful result will be produced, for whom, and for what decision?
- **Trigger:** What starts the work, and is it one-off, recurring, or event-driven?
- **Inputs:** Which documents, data, or systems are required, and who owns their quality?
- **Surface:** Which ChatGPT capability is the smallest one that can safely do the job?
- **Instructions:** What process, standards, examples, and constraints must always be followed?
- **Access:** Which data and actions are permitted, and which are prohibited?
- **Verification:** Which claims, citations, calculations, and interpretations must be independently checked?
- **Approval:** Who reviews the output and who authorizes any external action?
- **Failure:** When must ChatGPT stop, retry, revert, ask, or escalate?
- **Evidence:** What inputs, versions, decisions, approvals, and outputs must be logged?
- **Measure:** How will time, quality, rework, cost, confidence, adoption, and risk compare with the baseline?
- **Owner:** Who is accountable for maintenance, review, incidents, and retirement?

## Responsible Use Check Before Sharing or Acting

Apply the **S-A-F-E-R** check:

- **Source:** Can important claims be traced to credible, current, and relevant evidence?
- **Accuracy:** Have facts, figures, quotations, citations, calculations, and assumptions been checked?
- **Fairness:** Could the result exclude, stereotype, disadvantage, or misrepresent a person or group?
- **Exposure:** Does the interaction disclose confidential, personal, copyrighted, or restricted information?
- **Responsibility:** Is a named person accountable for the decision, external action, and correction of errors?

The required review should increase with uncertainty, data sensitivity, action autonomy, and potential impact. When the consequences of an error are unclear, pause and escalate instead of expanding ChatGPT’s authority.

## Custom GPT Quality Checklist

Before sharing a custom GPT, confirm that it has:

1. A narrow purpose and clearly defined users.
2. Supported tasks, prohibited tasks, and an escalation path.
3. Structured instructions with observable steps and acceptance criteria.
4. Curated, current, and appropriately licensed knowledge files.
5. Only the capabilities and external access necessary for its purpose.
6. Normal, ambiguous, incomplete, conflicting, sensitive, and adversarial test cases.
7. Required citations, uncertainty statements, and human approval rules.
8. A named owner, version history, review date, feedback channel, and retirement process.
