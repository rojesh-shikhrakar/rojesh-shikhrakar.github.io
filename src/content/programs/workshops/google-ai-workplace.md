## Learning Objectives

By the end of the workshop, participants will be able to:

- Explain how Gemini supports Google Workspace and anticipate common strengths, limitations, and failure modes.
- Write and improve prompts using relevant context, sources, constraints, examples, and acceptance criteria.
- Choose appropriately among the Gemini app, in-app Gemini assistance, Gems, Gemini Notebook, templates, AppSheet, and Apps Script.
- Use AI effectively across Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet, Chat, Calendar, Forms, Sites, Keep, Tasks, and Vids.
- Build source-grounded research and knowledge workflows with traceable evidence.
- Analyze workplace data while independently checking formulas, assumptions, charts, and conclusions.
- Design cross-app workflows with clear sources of truth, permissions, approvals, and failure handling.
- Apply privacy, security, fairness, accessibility, copyright, transparency, and accountability principles.
- Measure productivity and quality improvements without sacrificing trust, collaboration, or professional judgment.

## A Practical Prompt Framework

Use **G-C-S-C-E-F** as a flexible starting point:

1. **Goal:** State the outcome and why it matters.
2. **Context:** Explain the audience, situation, background, and relevant definitions.
3. **Sources:** Identify the files, messages, data, or evidence Gemini should use.
4. **Constraints:** Define policies, exclusions, tone, length, deadline, and boundaries.
5. **Evaluation:** Describe what a good result must include and what must be checked.
6. **Format:** Specify the required structure, such as a table, email, brief, formula, or slide outline.

## The Cross-Workspace Workflow Canvas

Every capstone workflow should answer:

- **Outcome:** What useful professional result should the workflow produce?
- **Trigger:** What starts it, and how often does it run?
- **Apps:** Which Workspace applications and Gemini surfaces are involved?
- **Sources:** Which files, messages, meetings, events, or datasets are authoritative?
- **Access:** Who can view or change each source, output, and intermediate artifact?
- **AI role:** Is Gemini retrieving, summarizing, drafting, transforming, analyzing, recommending, or acting?
- **Human role:** Who supplies context, checks the work, decides, approves, and communicates?
- **Verification:** Which facts, formulas, dates, citations, names, and assumptions must be checked?
- **Approval:** Which messages, records, publications, or external actions require confirmation?
- **Failure:** When should the workflow stop, retry, revert, or escalate?
- **Evidence:** What should be retained to make the workflow reviewable and auditable?
- **Measure:** How will time, quality, rework, adoption, confidence, and risk compare with the baseline?

## Responsible Google AI Check

Apply the **S-H-A-R-E** check before using or distributing AI-assisted work:

- **Source:** Is the output grounded in authoritative, current, and permitted information?
- **Human:** Is a named person responsible for reviewing the output and owning the decision?
- **Accuracy:** Have facts, formulas, citations, names, dates, and commitments been checked?
- **Rights:** Are privacy, confidentiality, permissions, copyright, consent, fairness, and accessibility protected?
- **Exposure:** Is the audience, sharing setting, recipient list, and external action appropriate?

Higher-impact work requires stronger evidence, more testing, tighter permissions, and more senior human review. If the consequences of an error or disclosure are unclear, pause and escalate.

## Capstone Challenge (Optional)

Participants redesign one recurring workplace process using two or more Google Workspace apps. The final presentation includes:

1. The current process, users, friction, and baseline effort.
2. The proposed Google AI workflow and expected professional value.
3. The apps, sources of truth, prompts, Gems, notebooks, or automations involved.
4. The flow of data and the required account, file, and app permissions.
5. Human responsibilities, approval gates, prohibited actions, and escalation paths.
6. A test set covering normal, incomplete, conflicting, sensitive, and adversarial inputs.
7. Evidence checks for facts, formulas, citations, dates, names, and commitments.
8. Risks involving privacy, security, bias, accessibility, copyright, and oversharing.
9. Success metrics, ownership, maintenance, and a 30-day pilot plan.
