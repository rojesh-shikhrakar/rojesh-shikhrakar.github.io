For decades, the primary benchmark for AI success was accuracy. In contained environments, a 99% precision rate could be treated as the gold standard. As AI enters healthcare, legal systems, and critical infrastructure, accuracy alone is no longer enough. We require **alignment**.

## The interpretability crisis

Modern neural networks are frequently described as black boxes. Their outputs can be precise while the path taken to reach them remains opaque—even to the engineers who built the system. That opacity creates friction in high-stakes decisions.

To bridge the gap, we need an editorial approach to AI architecture: systems should provide not only an answer but a coherent, human-readable rationale. This is not merely an interface challenge. It is structural groundwork for safety.

## From assistance to autonomy

The transition from AI as a tool to AI as an agent requires redesigned feedback loops. When an AI acts on a person’s behalf, the margin for error shrinks significantly.

- **Defined operational boundaries:** hard limits on decision-making autonomy.
- **Real-time rationalization:** understandable explanations for high-impact actions.
- **Human escalation:** automatic hand-off triggers for ambiguous or risky cases.

Trust is created when people can understand a system’s role, challenge its reasoning, and recover when it is wrong. Those qualities must be designed into the architecture—not added as documentation afterward.

> The goal of responsible autonomy is not to remove people from decisions. It is to give people better leverage without giving up meaningful control.
